Concept sketch · 26 Sep 2026

Every creature is a habit.

What Ronki looks like with a Pokémon heart. The kid still does the real thing in real life. What changes: each habit they are building shows up as a creature that comes closer to Ronki's fire, joins the team, grows up, and one day moves out into the world because it isn't needed anymore.

Verdict: yes, it works, and it fits better than it looks. Pokémon evolution maps almost one to one onto how a habit matures: with help, then alone, then without thinking. And the last stage lets the creature leave, which is exactly Ronki's promise of stepping aside. It also pulls four half-built Ronki systems into one spine instead of adding a fifth.
Two friends at the fire, two shadows at the edge
The whole idea in three verbs

Befriend it by doing. Grow it by doing it alone. Let it go.

Befriend

No throwing, no luck. A creature's shadow waits at the edge of the Nest. Each time the kid does the matching real thing, it comes one step closer. After a handful of times it sits at the fire and joins the team.

Grow

The creature changes shape when the habit changes shape. It grows up when a parent says "macht das jetzt allein". Counts alone never evolve anything, so tapping doesn't cheat it.

Let go

Final form: the creature moves out into the world map. The slot frees up for the next habit. The book of moved-out creatures becomes a record of growing up: "Mit 6: Zähne allein."

Pokémon on the left, Ronki on the right

A shadow at the edge

Starts when the family picks it
2 · Befreundet

Sits at the fire

Five real brushings, one paw step each. Then the hold-still moment and it joins the team.

5 times done (age 4 to 7)
3 · Groß

Grows up

The parent area asks once a week: "Putzt Louis inzwischen allein?" A yes changes its shape.

Parent says: allein
4 · Ausgezogen

Moves out

After about eight more weeks of "allein" it flies to the world map. Ronki stops asking about teeth.

~8 weeks alone, then the task leaves the Nest

Why eight weeks: the best-known study on habit forming (Lally and colleagues, 2010) found a median of 66 days until a behaviour runs on autopilot, with a wide range of 18 to 254. So stage 3 should be a parent-adjustable default, not a promise. The important part is step 4: the task leaves the Nest. That is the fade built into the loop.

The battle part, reframed

Trouble creatures: you calm them, and then they're friends

Pokémon needs conflict. Ronki's conflict is inside the kid's day, so the "wild" creatures are the things that make routines hard. There is no fight and no losing. Each calm evening makes the creature a little softer.

Flimmerfux

Shows up when a screen is on too long. Gets sleepy when the kid switches off on their own.

Calmed by: screen off without a fight

Schnarchling

Pulls the blanket back over your head. Turns into a morning buddy when getting up gets easier.

Calmed by: up on the first call

Wusselwicht

Hides one sock every morning. Becomes the tidy-up helper once the school bag is packed the night before.

Calmed by: bag packed in the evening
Across all ages

One loop, three registers

The befriend, grow, let go loop holds from kindergarten to teens. The voice, the art and who picks the habits change.

4 to 7

Das Nest

  • Ronki speaks every line, pictures only
  • 3 team slots, parent picks with the kid
  • Routines: teeth, dressing, bag, bedtime
  • Befriend after 5 times
8 to 11

Die Expedition

  • Kid reads and picks their own next creature
  • Up to 6 slots, all six life areas
  • Skills with badges: bike, swimming, cooking eggs
  • World map fills with moved-out creatures
  • Befriend after 7 to 10 times
12+

Das Feldbuch

  • Teen invents their own creature for a goal they set: sleep, training, study, phone time
  • Parent drops out of the loop, teen confirms "allein" themselves
  • Ronki becomes a quiet narrator

Honest read: a 13-year-old will not use a baby-dragon app. This band needs its own look (sketchbook, line art). Same engine, different skin. Treat it as phase 3 or not at all.

Guardrails

What we take from Pokémon, and what never comes in

Pokémon is built to keep you playing. Ronki is built to be needed less. Every borrowed mechanic has to pass that test.

Take

  • Silhouettes you want to fill. Curiosity about what's behind the next shadow.
  • A capped team. Few habits at once, by design.
  • Visible growth. The creature looks different when the kid is different.
  • Named, lovable creatures. The kid talks about Glutfunke at dinner, not about "the teeth task".
  • Mentors with a skill. The Freunde arcs already do this.

Leave out

  • Luck and rarity. No random encounters, no shinies, no drop rates. That's a loot box for a 6-year-old.
  • Catch 'em all. The book per age band stays small (about 20). Moving out is the goal, a full book isn't.
  • Fighting other kids. No PvP, no leaderboards, no trading between families.
  • Time pressure. No limited events, no "today only", and a creature never walks away because a day was missed. It just waits.
  • Anything that is Pokémon's. No balls, no names, no look-alikes. Our words: befriend, fire, Wesenbuch.

The real risk: when kids start doing a thing for the reward, the reward can crowd out their own reasons (researchers call it the overjustification effect). Two things keep it in check here. The creature mirrors what the kid did instead of paying for it: there is no currency to spend. And the reward leaves on its own once the habit is automatic, which is the moment it would start to do harm.

How it fits the Finch pass

What I'd do next

Test one creature before drawing twenty

A two-week test with Louis

  1. One shadow, one habit Louis doesn't own yet (evening teeth or bag packing), behind a feature switch.
  2. Build only the shadow, the paw steps, the hold-still moment and one team slot. Existing creature art is fine for the test.
  3. Watch two things: does he mention the creature without the app open, and does the task get easier or does he start bargaining for the creature.
  4. If he talks about it and doesn't bargain, draw the first three lines in the Bilderbuch style and build the parent "allein?" step.
Your call 1: catch or befriend?I wrote "befriend" everywhere. It's gentler and keeps us away from Pokémon's words. "Fangen" sells better to kids.
Your call 2: does the task really leave?Moving out removes the habit from the Nest. Some parents will want the reminder to stay. Default off, with a parent switch?
Your call 3: is 12+ in scope?The loop holds, the look doesn't. I'd keep Ronki at 4 to 11 and treat teens as a separate product later.
